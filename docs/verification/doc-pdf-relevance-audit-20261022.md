# DOC PDF relevance review — 22 October 2026

I reviewed the 51 Department of Conservation PDF records in the public publication catalogue: 19 historical periodical issues and 32 reports or research articles. The inclusion test is substantive source-text use or discussion of GIS, geospatial data, mapping, GPS, remote sensing, spatial analysis or spatial distribution. Place names and incidental GIS citations inside an unrelated bibliography do not qualify.

The 19 periodical issues remain in scope. Eighteen have exact original-text excerpts in `docs/verification/doc-science-newsletter-gis-evidence-20261018.json`. Conservation Science Newsletter issues 28–29 are documented separately as proposing a GIS database of fire-sensitive habitats in `docs/research/doc-and-professional-magazine-gis-sources-20261018.md`. All are dated before the 31 December 2025 cutoff and are locally hosted with checksums and first-page covers.

Of the 32 standalone DOC PDFs, 31 have a substantive geospatial connection in the original text, including GIS-based wildlife and archaeological analysis, spatial models, GIS-ready species distributions, GPS fieldwork, mapping, remote sensing, geodatabases and marine spatial-planning datasets. Two records now state their connection more precisely:

- The beach-vehicle bibliography says recent studies used drone imagery, GIS spatial analysis and remote sensing; it includes a study measuring the area and intensity of vehicle damage with GIS. The public title and description now reflect that scope.
- The mudfish review recommends mapping small-scale wetland and agricultural-drain distributions in a format suitable for GIS databases. Its description now presents this as a recommendation, not as completed GIS work.

The visitor-research bibliography was excluded. Its stated purpose is to catalogue visitor and recreation research; GIS appears only within individual cited studies, so the document itself is outside the geospatial scope. Its catalogue entry, locally hosted PDF, cover image and resulting public chapter reader links were removed.

The remaining 50 DOC PDFs are therefore in scope: 19 periodicals and 31 standalone works.
