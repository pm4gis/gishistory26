# DOC PDF relevance review — 22 October 2026

I reviewed the 51 Department of Conservation PDF records in the public publication catalogue: 19 periodical records (one combined PDF covers issues 28 and 29) and 32 standalone reports or research articles. The inclusion test is substantive source-text use or discussion of GIS, geospatial data, mapping, GPS, remote sensing, spatial analysis or spatial distribution. Place names and incidental GIS citations inside an unrelated bibliography do not qualify.

Of the 18 periodical records still listed, 17 first- and second-tranche acquisition records have machine-extracted original-text snippets in `docs/verification/doc-science-newsletter-gis-evidence-20261018.json`. The separate combined PDF for Conservation Science Newsletter issues 28–29 is documented as proposing a GIS database of fire-sensitive habitats in `docs/research/doc-and-professional-magazine-gis-sources-20261018.md`. All currently retained records are dated before the 31 December 2025 cutoff and their original PDF/checksum/cover checks must be re-run on the amended candidate.

Of the 32 standalone DOC PDFs, 31 have a substantive geospatial connection in the original text, including GIS-based wildlife and archaeological analysis, spatial models, GIS-ready species distributions, GPS fieldwork, mapping, remote sensing, geodatabases and marine spatial-planning datasets. Two records now state their connection more precisely:

- The beach-vehicle bibliography says recent studies used drone imagery, GIS spatial analysis and remote sensing; it includes a study measuring the area and intensity of vehicle damage with GIS. The public title and description now reflect that scope.
- The mudfish review recommends mapping small-scale wetland and agricultural-drain distributions in a format suitable for GIS databases. Its description now presents this as a recommendation, not as completed GIS work.

The visitor-research bibliography and Rare Bits 47 were excluded. Its stated purpose is to catalogue visitor and recreation research; GIS appears only within individual cited studies, so the document itself is outside the geospatial scope. Its catalogue entry, regional acquisition candidate, locally hosted PDF, cover image and resulting public chapter reader links were removed, preventing CI from reacquiring the out-of-scope document.

The updated catalogue has 49 DOC PDF records (18 periodicals, including the combined issues 28–29, and 31 standalone works). This is an inventory figure, not a claim that each retained issue meets the editorial relevance threshold. Original-page checks on Conservation Science Newsletter issues 20, 26, 33, 38 and 49 raise additional concerns; they remain subject to complete-issue checks and a separate preservation-aware decision. Rare Bits 47 has been removed from this candidate branch after checking page 16: a passing GPS mention does not justify listing the whole threatened-species issue as a GIS publication.
