# Timeline documentary source decisions, wave 5
Prepared 10 October 2026 from live original-image records, canonical repository metadata and authoritative institutional descriptions. The complete event audit is generated by `scripts/audit-timeline-image-coverage-wave5.mjs`.

## First NZMS 1 sheet, N134, 1939

The original **1939 *Napier and Hastings* sheet N134** was drawn by A. J. Reid and published by the Department of Lands and Survey as the first sheet of the national NZMS 1 one-inch series. The National Library holds the edition as `MapColl 830cba/1939-/15175`. National Library exhibition: https://natlib.govt.nz/exhibitions/unfolding-the-map/topographical-mapping . The original is documented as compiled using survey data and aerial photography with contours at hundred-foot intervals.

A digital copy of the **1943 revision of N134** exists separately at https://natlib.govt.nz/records/23363272 and via the library's archival delivery endpoint. The catalogue explicitly calls the previous edition 1939, identifies the 1943 revision and allows redistribution of the 1943 digital image, with the attribution *Sourced from LINZ. Crown Copyright reserved.* This does not demonstrate that the download is the 1939 printing. Te Ara separately displays the first 1939 sheet, but flags image-specific reproduction restrictions: https://teara.govt.nz/en/zoomify/18907/first-topographical-map-of-hawkes-bay .

At the time of this batch an original binary with verified identity as **1939** has not been acquired through an ordinary authorised access route. The National Library site has returned an automated-access challenge in attempted retrieval. No circumvention or substitution has been used. The 1939 event continues to show an explicitly labelled *different-series* 1955 NZMS 13 illustration until a correct source image is available. Its detail panel now links to both the 1939 holding and 1943 revision.

## Original first NZMS 260, 1977

The **1977 T12 Thames** first-edition NZMS 260 sheet is held by the National Library at https://natlib.govt.nz/records/35866010 . The catalogue identifies the 1:50,000 metric map, New Zealand Map Grid projection, first edition 1977, reprint 1978, and an authorised digital image. It permits redistribution of the 1977 scan with *Sourced from LINZ. Crown Copyright reserved* attribution; the separate 1978 reprint is credited to the New Zealand Cartographic Society. The full map original was not retrieved from the catalogue's mediated delivery service. The event links to the correct 1977 original; its existing 2020 Topo50 image remains explicitly labelled as a later comparison.

## Original Canterbury documentary media

Four Commons files were selected after reviewing their subjects, date descriptions and individual licence declarations.

* **Seismic hazard map around epicentre of 2010 Canterbury earthquake**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seismic_hazard_map_around_epicentre_of_2010_Canterbury_earthquake.jpg . USGS / Global Seismic Hazard Assessment Program, public domain, displayed 3 September 2010. This is a *probabilistic hazard* map showing a 10% chance of peak-ground-acceleration exceedance in 50 years, not an observed intensity map or an emergency GIS screen. It is a contextual background to the 2010 Common Operating Picture event.
* **Greendale Fault (44)**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Greendale_Fault_(44).jpeg . Brendan Zim photograph, dated 4 September 2010, of earthquake-related surface displacement. Commons verified CC BY 2.0 and identifies the earlier Flickr image. This is direct photographic evidence of the event’s ground effects, distinct from the technology used to map them.
* **Cashmere High School aerial photograph, 24 February 2011**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cashmere_High_School_aerial_photo,_24_Feb_2011.jpg . NZ Aerial Mapping for the Christchurch Response Centre. Licensed CC BY 3.0 NZ. Contemporary earthquake-response orthophotography with liquefaction effects, captured two days after the Christchurch earthquake. It supports the existing event photograph archive as a supplementary image.
* **Napier06**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Napier06.jpg . NASA World Wind screenshot composite of Napier, Hastings and Cape Kidnappers, uploaded 18 March 2006 and public domain. A contemporary alternative electronic globe, **not** a Google Maps/New Zealand 2007 launch image. It is linked only as supplementary context on the 2007 web-mapping event panel, never as a Google Maps button background.

The acquisition script checks the *live* Commons file-level short licence and retrieves the actual original from upload.wikimedia.org. It stores SHA-256, width and height, original, WebP display, WebP thumbnail, caption, credit and licence evidence. If acquisition fails for any item, publication is held. No image originates from an AI image generator.

## Other high-priority work

The accepted original subject gaps remain MAPPAK/PDP-11, NZMS 290/NZLRI, early Intergraph, early New Zealand ARC/INFO, 1990s council GIS, Landonline’s original interface, DOCgis, LRIS Portal and S-map’s 2011 interface. Those should remain original-image research priorities. A publicly viewable Manaaki Whenua S-map help screenshot exists (https://smap.landcareresearch.co.nz/support/learn-to-use-this-tool/mapping-tool-introduction), but the site's licence is BY-NC-ND 3.0 NZ, which does not permit publishing a modified image derivative under the default WebP workflow without further analysis or approval. It was therefore **not copied** for this batch. See https://smap.landcareresearch.co.nz/terms-of-use .

The resulting image coverage CSV separately identifies direct event subjects, contextual card illustrations, and supporting images without a button background. Each published image retains its full attribution on the individual image-detail page. Existing event IDs, passages, captions and routes remain unchanged.
