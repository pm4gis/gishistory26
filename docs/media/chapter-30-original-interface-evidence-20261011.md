### Reuse decision

The original 2003 DOCgis image is a concrete discovery, but is hosted inside an Esri conference proceedings PDF. Although DOC's general [copyright page](https://www.doc.govt.nz/footer-links/copyright/) says some Crown-owned DOC material is reusable under CC BY 4.0, that statement does not establish ownership and reusable licensing for an image inside a third-party conference publication. The image could contain Esri interface elements, map layers or third-party copyright. The DOC science publications guidance separately describes requesting republication permission. [DOC copyright guidance](https://www.doc.govt.nz/about-us/science-publications/about-our-science-publications/) should be consulted for any formal request. Link to the full publisher PDF and the exact source figure; refrain from copying, cropping, producing derivative WebPs, or creating a misleading image-detail page.

The existing reader now places a discrete, contextual source link immediately beside the DOCgis paragraph `ch30-s09-p01`, identifying Figure 9 and linking to the original page of the PDF. The link explicitly states that local reproduction permission is unresolved. This is an authentic historical image *reference*, **not** a new locally published image. The 2019 licensed Wikidata/New Zealand libraries screenshot remains labelled as a later example. Published image counts and provenance should remain unchanged.

### Rights-clearing information for the owner

Ask the Department of Conservation's rights/publications team and the Esri proceedings rights holder to identify the copyright owner(s) of **Figure 9** in the 2003 paper and whether the original DOCgis Beta HTML 2.0 screenshot, excluding any third-party content, may be reproduced on the publicly accessible not-for-profit `history.pm4gis.nz` website. For the other candidates, obtain images and applicable permissions from the organisations identified above. Do not send messages without the project owner's explicit direction.
