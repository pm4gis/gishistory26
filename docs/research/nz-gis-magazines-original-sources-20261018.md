## Other historical primary evidence

- Keith Carr, The implementation of GIS in New Zealand, in UN ESCAP's 1988 GIS proceedings. A contemporaneous account of NZLRI digitisation, DCDB development and interagency coordination. https://repository.unescap.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12870/7966/ESCAP-1988-RP-GIS-geographic-information-systems.pdf . Record and investigate the rights of the original full proceedings before copying.
- Department of Conservation's 3 October 2011 interview with GIS Champion Paul Hughes, describing Treaty settlements and biodiversity information management, and his earlier career selling GIS in the oil industry. https://blog.doc.govt.nz/2011/10/03/gis-hughes/
- Department of Conservation's 2011 interview with spatial analyst Richard Earl, describing national ecosystem and species distribution modelling. https://blog.doc.govt.nz/page/398/
- Department of Conservation's 4 November 2013 article GIS: From Kerbal to conservation, authored by Duane Wilkins, recording GIS Day outreach, satellite imagery and Landsat 8 at DOC. https://blog.doc.govt.nz/tag/gis/ . This is a potentially important missing first-person DOC/GIS historical source, to be integrated carefully once checked against existing manuscript material.

## Ministry for the Environment source recovery

The following official 2017 document has a verified original PDF URL that carries a cache-version query: https://environment.govt.nz/assets/Publications/Files/Discussion-Paper-F-Mapping-Standards.pdf?v=3b59d7c9a4bf180ab8396f11478c063acd7fc73d . It discusses GIS ePlans, 266 spatial layers in 12 regional plans, and inconsistent online map viewers. Earlier unversioned URLs entered redirect loops on GitHub-hosted runners. Alternative links with an original publisher-provided version parameter are included in `src/lib/historical-gis-new-candidates-20261018.mjs` where obtainable. Also examine https://environment.govt.nz/publications/considering-a-future-spatial-framework-for-wetland-mapping-and-monitoring-in-new-zealand/ (2018), where an official PDF download is advertised.

The copyright information for NIWA differs by publication and date. The 2002 whole-issue Water & Atmosphere PDF requests editor permission before reprinting, notwithstanding the later NIWA website's general Creative Commons terms. The full originals should not be copied without a clear grant for the specific issue. See https://webstatic.niwa.co.nz/library/W%26A10%283%29.pdf and https://niwa.co.nz/about-niwa/terms. No public-hosting language should be added to the website itself.

Bibliography and exact source URLs are preserved to enable later source-based updates to chapters and people/organisation records. No new post-2025 historical entries arise from this research.
